Reviewing the structure of student papers

A way to mark up a student’s paper draft at the level of structure, not wording. The Claude Code skill paper-structure-review produces a reverse outline and a table matching claims to evidence.

View the skill (GitHub)

The skill is written in Japanese and its output is in Japanese, but it reads English drafts as well.

The problem

What needs fixing in a student draft is usually its structure rather than typos or phrasing: the order of sections, claims that their evidence does not support, discussion in the wrong place. But when you read from the top and mark as you go, your eyes are pulled to the wording. Structural defects are hard to see one paragraph at a time.

The idea

Separate the layers

Mixing wording (typos, phrasing, LaTeX) with structure (logic, section order, support for claims) leaves both half done. This skill does structure only. Wording goes to a separate review file, and any overlap is tagged “[already in the wording review]”.

Let the student do the rewriting

The skill stops at a list of instructions and never touches the text. The student first fixes the outline, gets it approved, and only then rewrites the paper. The order is there so that the student, not the tool, learns to build an argument.

Output

The skill writes memo/structure_review.md next to the draft, always in four parts.

Table 1. Parts of structure_review.md
PartContents
1. Reverse outlineEach paragraph’s claim compressed to one sentence, numbered. Repetition, leaps, misordering and empty sections stand out
2. Claim-evidence tableFor each claim, where the supporting experiment, figure or citation is, and a verdict: unsupported, contradictory or overstated
3. Structural instructionsNumbered S1, S2, … by priority. Each says which paragraph, where to, and why
4. How to useHow the teacher and the student work with the document

A claim-evidence table looks like this (a fictional example):

Table 2. Example claim-evidence table
#Claim (where)EvidenceVerdict
AThe method works with little data (Sec. 1 ¶3)None in the textUnsupported. Add an experiment varying data size, or weaken the claim
BAccuracy equal to or better than the baseline (abstract)Table 3: 0.91 vs 0.93Overstated. The number is below the baseline
CComputation time halved (Sec. 5)Table 4OK, but missing from the contribution list in Sec. 1

Defects it looks for

The skill carries a checklist of patterns that keep appearing in student papers.

  • The result stressed most in the abstract and conclusion is not in the contribution list. Or a listed contribution is never shown in the experiments.
  • The introduction promises something the body never supports.
  • The premises in the introduction contradict the experimental conclusions or limitations.
  • A number lower than the baseline is described as “comparable or better”.
  • Sections that set something up but never connect to the conclusion; experiments tied to no claim.
  • New discussion buried in the conclusion instead of the discussion section.
  • The same content explained twice, in the method and the experiment sections.
  • Paragraphs that do not start from known information, so a topic appears with no link to the previous paragraph.

How we use it

  1. Teacher: approves, rejects or edits S1…Sn, writing directly in structure_review.md.
  2. Student: applies the approved instructions to the reverse outline first, gets the revised outline checked, and only then rewrites the text.
  3. Typos and phrasing follow the separate wording review.

On later rounds, the existing structure_review.md is not overwritten; only resolved and new issues are updated. For drafts synced between Overleaf and git, memo/structure_review.md can be shared with the student while the teacher’s private notes stay local.

What it must not do

  • Edit the paper (.tex or .docx). Its only output is structure_review.md.
  • Invent evidence to fill the table. If there is none, it writes “none in the text”, which is often the most important finding.
  • Give bare orders. Each instruction says why the structure is a problem, and open questions come with two options.

Setup

Put SKILL.md in ~/.claude/skills/paper-structure-review/. In the draft’s folder, ask Claude Code to “review the structure of this paper” or “make a reverse outline”, and it will use the skill. LaTeX drafts can be one file or split with \input. For Word drafts, the text is extracted first.
